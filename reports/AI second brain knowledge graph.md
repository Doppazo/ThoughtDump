# Structure Notes Now, Graph Them Later

A knowledge graph shows your notes as dots, with lines for the connections between them. Thought Dump's graph will only mean something if the AI follows a few strict note-writing rules from the start. Drawing the graph itself is the easy part. The rules are: one idea per note, titles written as specific phrases, categories and people stored as **links** rather than plain text, a short list of tags reused from an approved list, and at most about three AI-suggested links per note, each with a stated reason and each easy to undo. This matters because Obsidian (the free notes app whose file format we copy) draws lines between notes **only from links**. Folders never show up in the graph, and tags show up only if you switch them on ([Obsidian Help, Graph view](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/graph)). So today's `category: Business Ideas` adds nothing to the graph. On the app side, the evidence says **don't add a fifth "Graph" tab**. Whole-collection graphs are widely seen as eye candy and work badly on phones, and competitors such as Capacities and Anytype leave them out of their phone apps ([Capacities docs](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/graph-view); [Anytype Community](https://community.anytype.io/t/add-graph-view-to-mobile/5735)). Put a small "Connections" view on each note instead, and later add a "Map" mode inside the Brain tab that shows themes. For the hackathon, skip logins and keep everything on the device. Add Supabase (a hosted database with logins and file storage, free tier included) afterwards. The biggest demo wins come from voice apps that make the AI's work visible and trustworthy: color-coded cards from one dump, a "source" button that plays the original audio, and Ask answers that cite the notes they used.

## One idea per note, titled like a claim, makes a good dot

Note-taking experts agree that the best note holds **exactly one "building block"**: one concept, argument, observation or task. The size depends on the idea, not on a word count ([Zettelkasten.de atomicity guide](https://zettelkasten.de/atomicity/guide/)). The same guide offers practical tests. The note should be easy to title, readable at a glance, and break if you remove anything. It also allows one exception: a problem and its solution can share a note when a future reader needs them together. Andy Matuschak, a well-known researcher on note systems, argues that notes should be "atomic, concept-oriented, and densely linked," and that **titles work like handles**. A sharp title such as a full claim lets other notes point to the idea by name alone ([Matuschak, Evergreen note titles are like APIs](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Evergreen_note_titles_are_like_APIs)).

For Thought Dump, the files in /raw are the "fleeting" captures: unprocessed source material. The notes in /ideas, /thoughts and /todos are the real dots on the graph. The AI splitter should aim for one idea, question or task per note. It should not split down to single sentences, and it should not keep a whole dump as one note. Titles should be specific ("Campus laundry pickup could run on student couriers"), not generic labels ("Laundry idea"). Specific titles make readable graph labels, clear link targets, and better matches when the app scans other notes for unlinked mentions of a title. One honest counterpoint: Obsidian's CEO, Steph Ango, refuses to have AI write his notes ("Don't delegate understanding") ([Steph Ango, How I use Obsidian](https://stephango.com/vault)). Thought Dump's answer is to keep every AI decision visible and editable, which the section on auto-linking below covers.

## Links draw the graph; folders and tags only sort and filter it

The most important technical fact is what creates a line in the graph. In Obsidian, **only `[[wikilinks]]` create lines between notes**. A wikilink is a note title in double square brackets. Notes with more incoming links appear as bigger dots. Tags can appear as optional extra dots through a toggle, and folders never appear at all. Folders can still color dots or hide them through search filters such as `path:raw` ([Obsidian Help, Graph view](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/graph)). Wikilinks inside the YAML frontmatter (the block of `key: value` settings at the top of each file) also draw lines in current Obsidian versions. A June 2026 forum request asks for a way to turn this off because category links clutter the per-note graph ([Obsidian Forum #114940](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/an-option-to-disable-frontmatter-links-to-show-up-in-graph-view/114940)). That makes storing categories as links in frontmatter powerful, but it can also make the graph noisy. Thought Dump's own graph should let users hide "property links" separately from links in the note body.

Steph Ango keeps almost all of his notes in one place and organizes them through a `categories` property that holds links to category notes. He also uses **plural names for categories and tags** and `YYYY-MM-DD` dates everywhere ([Steph Ango, How I use Obsidian](https://stephango.com/vault)). Nick Milo's "Maps of Content" idea is similar. A Map of Content is a hub note made mostly of links, created only once a topic gets messy enough to need one ([Knowledge Accumulation Substack](https://knowledgeaccumulation.substack.com/p/how-to-create-a-map-of-contents-moc)). Hub notes like these give a graph its natural cluster centers. PARA, Tiago Forte's system of sorting by Projects, Areas, Resources and Archives, organizes by how actionable something is ([Forte Labs](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)). It fits our to-do list better than our graph, so at most it could become an optional `project` link later.

Obsidian's rules for the frontmatter fields (it calls them "properties") are strict, and our AI has to follow them exactly. Each property name has **one type across the whole vault**, meaning the whole collection of notes. Lists use one dash per line. Dates are stored as `2020-08-21` and date-times as `2020-08-21T10:30:00`. **Links inside properties must be in quotes** ([Obsidian Help, Properties](https://obsidian.md/help/properties)). Unquoted links break the whole frontmatter block ([Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/internal-links-in-frontmatter-do-work-without-quotes-after-all/91106)). Nested properties (a setting inside a setting) are not supported in Obsidian's editor, and the singular `tag`/`alias` property names are deprecated, with support dropped in Obsidian 1.9 ([Obsidian Help, Properties](https://obsidian.md/help/properties)). Tags can't contain spaces, must include at least one non-number character, and are case-insensitive ([Obsidian Help, Tags](https://obsidian.md/help/tags)). Titles must avoid `# | ^ : %% [[ ]]` or links can fail ([Obsidian Help, Internal links](https://obsidian.md/help/links)). Aliases (alternate names for a note, such as "AI" for "Artificial intelligence") "should always be formatted as a list" and are found by Obsidian's unlinked-mention search ([Obsidian Help, Aliases](https://obsidian.md/help/aliases)).

Together these give the rulebook below. Most rows come straight from the sources. A few are our own choices for a consistent AI, such as the `/hubs` folder and the reuse-first tag list.

| Element | Rule for our AI |
|---|---|
| **Folders** | Keep /raw, /audio, /ideas, /thoughts, /todos as type buckets for the app and for coloring. Add one /hubs folder for category and entity notes. Never rely on a folder to carry meaning, because the graph ignores folders. |
| **Titles = filenames** | Write a specific phrase or claim of 100 characters or fewer. Strip `# \| ^ : %% [ ]` and characters phones and Windows forbid in file names. Replace "Title: subtitle" colons with " - ". Titles must be unique across the vault; if one clashes, add a short date. Raw and audio files keep their timestamp names (`2026-10-25 1402 voice`). |
| **Tags** | Use 0–3 per note, lowercase, kebab-case (words joined with hyphens), plural, picked from the existing list first. Use tags only for filters that don't deserve their own note (`campus`, `urgent`). Never repeat the type or category as a tag. |
| **Categories** | Store as `categories:`, a list of quoted links to hub notes. A note can have more than one. Create the hub note once about 3 notes share the category. |
| **Entities** (people, projects, places) | Store as `people:`, `projects:` and `places:` lists of quoted links. Leave a one-off mention as plain text and create an entity note once the name recurs. |
| **Aliases** | Store as `aliases:`, a dash list on the entity or hub note ("Mom", "my mother", "Mum"). Match new mentions against it before creating anything new. |
| **Type** | Keep plain text (idea / thought / todo / raw), never a link. A link to an "Ideas" hub would connect everything into one giant knot. Use type for colors instead. |
| **Links** | Write up to 3 AI links per note in `## Related`, each with a specific reason. Zero is allowed. Don't add the reverse link on the other note, because Obsidian shows backlinks automatically. |
| **Duplicates** | A repeated idea becomes a new note linked "revisits [[X]] from DATE". A repeated to-do asks the user "already have this — duplicate?". Never merge automatically. |

### Before and after: our note format, fixed

Here is a typical current note, reconstructed from our spec:

```markdown
File: ideas/Laundry idea.md
---
type: idea
category: Business Ideas
tags: [campus, startup]
created: 2026-10-25T14:02
source: "[[2026-10-25 1402 voice]]"
done: false
example: true
---
Students could pay other students to pick up and drop off laundry. Talked to Prof. Lee about it.

## Related
- [[Dorm food delivery]]: similar
```

And here is the same note following the rules above:

```markdown
File: ideas/Campus laundry pickup could run on student couriers.md
---
type: idea
categories:
  - "[[Business Ideas]]"
tags:
  - campus
  - startups
people:
  - "[[Prof. Lee]]"
projects:
  - "[[Campus Laundry App]]"
aliases:
  - laundry pickup idea
created: 2026-10-25T14:02:00
source: "[[2026-10-25 1402 voice]]"
ai_generated: true
example: true
---
Students could pay other students to pick up and drop off laundry.
Talked to [[Prof. Lee]] about it after class.

## Related
<!-- ai-related:start -->
- [[Dorm food delivery could share the same courier pool]]: same student-courier model, different product (AI)
<!-- ai-related:end -->
```

Each change has a reason. The **title** is now a claim that can stand on its own as a dot in the graph. **`category` became `categories`**, a list of quoted links, so the note now joins the "Business Ideas" cluster. **Tags** became a dash list, because Obsidian rewrites inline `[a, b]` lists whenever someone edits them in its interface, which creates needless sync changes ([Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/issue-with-new-properties-display/66496/8)). **`created` gained seconds** to match the documented date-time format. (We could not confirm whether Obsidian recognizes the form without seconds.) **People and projects** are now links that connect this idea to other clusters. **`done` was dropped from the idea**; we suggest keeping it only on to-dos. **`ai_generated` is a flat yes/no flag**, not nested, because Obsidian can't edit nested properties. The **vague reason "similar" was replaced with a specific one**, and the AI's links sit between start/end markers so the app can regenerate or remove them in bulk. The Smart Linker plugin uses the same marker approach ([Smart Linker](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/smart-linker)). The quoted `source` link stays. It draws a line back to the raw capture, which is good for showing where a note came from, but it turns every raw note into a small hub. Give the graph a "hide raw sources" filter for that.

Audio embeds (`![[file.webm]]`, a link with `!` in front that plays the file inside the note) are fine as they are. `.webm` is a supported audio format, and attachment files show in the graph only when the "Attachments" toggle is on ([Obsidian Help, Accepted file formats](https://obsidian.md/help/file-formats); [Obsidian Help, Graph view](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/graph)). Our graph should hide them by default. Finally, whenever the app renames a note, it must rewrite every `[[link]]` that points to it, including links in properties and Related sections. Obsidian does this automatically ([Obsidian Help, Internal links](https://obsidian.md/help/links)), so a vault exported from our app has to stay consistent too.

## The AI suggests and the user can always undo: how auto-linking stays honest

Most AI note tools are **more cautious than Thought Dump plans to be**. Smart Connections shows related notes in a side panel without changing the notes themselves ([Obsidian community listing](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/smart-connections)). Reflect adds links only when the user asks for them ([Reflect blog](https://reflect.app/blog/automatically-add-backlinks-using-ai)). Mem suggests collections that the user accepts with one click or renames first ([Mem blog](https://get.mem.ai/blog/new-and-improved-collections)). Capacities' AI fills fields only "from objects you already have" ([Capacities docs](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/use-cases/network-of-people)). The research found no tool that silently writes AI links into note text by default. Thought Dump writes links automatically, so it needs the safety features those tools get from asking first: clear AI markers, reasons, and easy removal.

The research points to a three-step linking method. First, "embeddings" (number fingerprints of a note's meaning, used to find similar notes) pull the 10–20 most similar older notes. Second, Claude picks at most about three of them, gives a one-line reason for each, and may pick none. Third, people and projects are linked whenever their name or an alias appears, with no limit, because exact name matches are the most reliable kind of link. One linking plugin states that meaning-based suggestions "are more conservative and less reliable than title and alias matches" ([Semantic Auto-Linker](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/semantic-auto-linker)). Keeping the AI's choice list short also matters. In the iText2KG study, an AI that saw *all* known entities proposed implied connections with **81–83% precision**. Shown only the locally relevant ones, it stuck to stated connections and reached **90–94%** ([iText2KG, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.03284v1)). Similarity cutoffs vary by model: Smart Linker defaults to 5 links and a 0.75 similarity score ([Smart Linker](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/smart-linker)). In iText2KG, though, pairs of truly similar entities scored only **0.6 on average**, so a 0.7 cutoff misses many real matches. Treat any number as a starting point to test on real notes.

Consistency comes from giving the AI a fixed menu to choose from. The Simple Graph Builder plugin sends a strict JSON schema (a template the AI's answer must match) with every request and drops any answer that doesn't fit ([Simple Graph Builder](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/simple-graph-builder)). Tag plugins cap tags at 2–5 per note ([Smart Tagger](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/smart-tagger)). AI Tag Curator runs a "health check" that flags tags used only once, near-duplicate tags, and tags so broad they match everything ([AI Tag Curator](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/ai-tag-curator)). In practice, every time Claude processes a dump it should receive the existing categories with counts, the most-used tags, the list of people and projects with their aliases, and the candidate notes. It must reuse existing values when the meaning matches, flag anything new with `is_new: true`, and link only to note IDs from the list it was given. The server should then delete any link to an ID that doesn't exist, which stops the AI from inventing notes. Same people, different names ("Mom" versus "my mother") are handled by checking aliases first, then meaning similarity, then asking the AI to confirm. Simple Graph Builder uses this chain ([Simple Graph Builder](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/simple-graph-builder)). Truly ambiguous names, like two different people called "Sam," should be shown to the user rather than merged. Without this cleanup step, standard AI extraction leaves many duplicates: **up to 31% of entities in one test were false or duplicated** ([iText2KG](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.03284v1)).

Making notes correctable follows a consistent pattern. AI suggestions start unselected, with confidence and context shown ([Semantic Auto-Linker](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/semantic-auto-linker)). Tana lets users block the AI from specific fields ([Tana supertags docs](https://outliner.tana.inc/learn/features/supertags)). Thought Dump can adapt this without slowing down capture. Mark AI links with "(AI)". Once a user edits a field, treat it as theirs and never overwrite it when the AI reprocesses. Provide one-tap "remove link" and "wrong person" buttons, and remember rejected pairs so they aren't suggested again. On duplicates, no tool documents a reliable approach, so the safest rule is the one in the rulebook above. Saying the same idea twice is useful information, so link the new note to the old one instead of merging, and ask only about repeated to-dos.

## Put the graph inside each note, not in a fifth tab

Graph views are less useful than they look. In a long Obsidian forum thread, skeptics call the default graph meaningless ("chaotic patterns have no meaning"), while practical users say the per-note "local graph" is "much more intuitive" and helps mainly while writing ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/whats-the-point-of-the-graph-view-how-are-you-using-it/71316)). Writer Eleanor Konik, who defends graph views, uses hers for upkeep (spotting unconnected notes and sync errors) and for judging when a cluster is ready to become a draft. She does not use it for navigating ([Eleanor Konik](https://www.eleanorkonik.com/its-not-just-a-pretty-gimmick-in-defense-of-obsidians-graph-view)). The honest conclusion is that graphs help with **context** (what connects to this note?) and **diagnosis** (what's isolated?), not with browsing. In an app where the AI does the organizing, the upkeep use mostly disappears. What's left is "show me what this connects to" and "show me my themes and gaps." InfraNodus does the second by grouping concepts into clusters and pointing out "structural gaps" between them ([InfraNodus](https://infranodus.com/about/how-it-works)).

Phones make this harder. Obsidian users report that pinch-to-zoom grabs a dot instead of zooming. On Android you have to "carefully place 2 fingers in empty spaces" ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/pinch-to-pan-zoom-selects-a-node-if-a-finger-touches-one-in-the-graph-on-android/20683)), and vaults with 3,000+ notes crash and redraw on iOS ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/graph-view-keeps-crashing-and-re-drawing-in-ios-app/73719)). Capacities states that "Phone and tablet do not have a full space graph view," and its graph follows whatever note is open ([Capacities docs](https://docs.capacities.io/reference/graph-view)). Amplenote lists a mobile graph only as a plan ([Amplenote Help](https://www.amplenote.com/help/note_graph)).

So our recommendation comes in three steps. **First (hackathon):** each note's detail screen gets a "Connections" section with a small static graph showing one step out from the note, above a list of Links, Backlinks and AI-suggested connections, grouped by idea, thought and to-do. Tapping the small graph opens a full-screen version with a 1-or-2-step toggle. Tapping a dot shows a preview card, and tapping again opens the note. **Second (later):** add a Cards/Map switch inside the Brain tab. Map shows AI-computed theme clusters, colored by type and filtered by Brain's existing filters (type, Raw, "this week"), with "gap" prompts that jump to Ask. **Third:** Ask can return a small graph when someone asks "how are my notes on X connected?", and a weekly review can show "unconnected thoughts" as cards. Settings is the wrong home, because people rarely go there to explore. For touch, use these rules: two fingers always zoom even when touching a dot, dots can't be dragged on mobile, show at most about 50–150 dots, and freeze the layout once it settles. Constantly shifting positions are the top complaint. Any graph library handles a phone-sized graph. react-force-graph is quickest to set up, and Sigma.js is the choice if you ever need thousands of dots ([Cylynx comparison](https://www.cylynx.io/blog/a-comparison-of-javascript-graph-network-visualisation-libraries/)). None of these placement ideas has been user-tested for an AI-first app, so test the Connections list against the small graph with real users.

## Voice apps point to trust, cited answers and resurfacing as the winning features

The voice-note market falls into three groups. Some apps clean up a ramble into polished text: AudioPen offers a three-level rewrite dial ([Joseph Nilo review](https://josephnilo.com/blog/audiopen-review/)) and Letterly 25+ styles ([Kripesh Adwani](https://kripeshadwani.com/letterly-review/)). Some are "memory" apps with Ask-AI over everything you've recorded (Voicenotes, Mem, Limitless). Some are dictation tools that work in any text box (Wispr Flow, Superwhisper). Rewrite styles are now standard, so they won't make us stand out. The closest competitor is **Cleft Notes**, which saves Markdown directly into an Obsidian vault and shows summary, actions and transcript together ([cleftnotes.com](https://www.cleftnotes.com)). It produces one note per recording, not separate linked notes with a graph. **Mem 2.0's Voice Mode** "automatically determines your intent" and lets users accept the note or refine it with follow-up instructions ([Mem help](https://help.mem.ai/features/voice-mode.md)). That's a good model for "say 'make the second one a task for Friday.'" The category is getting attention. **Meta acquired Limitless in December 2025** and shut the service down in several regions with a deadline to export data ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/)), and **Amazon agreed to buy Bee in July 2025** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/22/amazon-acquires-bee-the-ai-wearable-that-records-everything-you-say/)). Those shutdowns make a strong pitch for Thought Dump: "your notes are plain files you own, downloadable as an Obsidian .zip."

The ideas most worth borrowing all build trust. Limitless's Ask answers link to the source entries ([yespress](https://yespress.io/products/limitless.md)), and Plaud promises "source-based answers" ([Plaud](https://eu.plaud.ai/products/plaud-notepin-s)). We should have Ask show tappable chips for the cards it used. Voicenotes stores the recording, transcript and key points together and promises notes are "never used to train AI" ([voicenotes.com](https://voicenotes.com)). Reflect, by contrast, deletes audio after processing ([Reflect Academy](https://reflect.academy/audio-recordings)), so our "keep the original" approach is a real difference. None of the apps reviewed clearly links each small note back to its exact moment in the audio. A "🎙 Source · 0:37" chip that plays from that timestamp would therefore stand out. It needs a speech-to-text service that returns word-level timestamps. Among OpenAI's models only **whisper-1** still does (about $0.006 per minute), while gpt-4o-mini-transcribe costs about $0.003 per minute without them ([ConvertAudioToText](https://convertaudiototext.com/blog/openai-whisper-api-pricing-2026)). Groq's Whisper has a free tier that doesn't need a card ([MorphLLM](https://www.morphllm.com/groq-pricing)). For keeping users coming back, Day One users call "On This Day" resurfacing "THE single best feature" ([Day One](https://dayoneapp.com/blog/streak-story-david-geveke/)), and Limitless sends a morning digest ([Limitless](https://feedback.limitless.ai/announcements/launched-daily-notifications-ask-ai-anything-on-desktop)). An "Echoes" card ("you said something like this 12 days ago") reuses the same embeddings that Ask needs. Things to avoid: always-on recording (Bee drew privacy criticism ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/amazon-buys-wearable-ai-startup-bee-a-wrist-device-that-hears-and-transcribes-every-word-you-speak))), meeting bots, and long lists of rewrite styles.

## Skip logins for the demo and add Supabase afterward

Accounts and cloud sync add work that judges won't see, so **keep the hackathon build on the device with no sign-in**. When you're ready for sync, the beginner-friendly default is **Supabase**. It bundles a database (Postgres), logins, file storage and pgvector (a database add-on that stores embeddings so Ask can search by meaning) in one service. The free plan includes a **500 MB database, 1 GB of file storage and 50,000 monthly users**, and Pro is a flat **$25 a month** ([Causo guide](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/supabase-vs-alternatives-founders-2026)). Supabase supports "magic link" sign-in, where users get an emailed link instead of a password, and Row-Level Security, a database rule that ensures each user sees only their own notes. In Thought Dump terms, audio goes in Storage, notes and links go in database tables, and embeddings go in pgvector. **Firebase** (Google) is easy for mobile and live updates, but its pay-as-you-go plan has **no spending cap by default**, so you would need budget alerts ([AlexCloudstar](https://www.alexcloudstar.com/blog/supabase-vs-firebase-2026/)). **Convex** suits teams writing everything in TypeScript who want screens that update themselves. It is usually paired with **Clerk** for logins, and Clerk's free tier rose to **50,000 monthly users** in February 2026 ([Clerk](https://clerk.com/articles/clerk-pricing-explained)). All of these prices come from third-party write-ups, so check the vendor pages before committing.

## Conclusion

The graph is an outcome, not a feature to build first. A graph from a weekend build will look impressive only if it has a few meaningful hubs: categories, people and projects joined by links with stated reasons. A thousand loosely connected dots will look like noise. So the team's leverage is in the prompt and the file format, not the drawing code. A strict list of options, a short candidate list and a three-link cap make the AI more reliable *and* the graph more readable at once. The research also shows that Thought Dump's riskiest choice, writing links automatically instead of suggesting them, is defensible only if every AI decision is labeled, explained and undoable with one tap.

There's also a positioning insight. Competitors split between rewriting your ramble and remembering everything you said. Few turn speech into small, linked notes that point back to the exact second you said them, saved as files you own. That combination of separate idea notes, provenance and portability is the story to tell the judges. The graph is the picture that shows it, kept small and placed beside each note. Two things are still unknown: how many links per note feels right to users, and whether a list or a small graph serves phone users better. Test both with real users rather than assuming.
